# Nyfasi China IP and manufacturing due diligence

**Case:** Experiment 001 — Nyfasi Deluxe Detangler  
**Review date:** 5 November 2026  
**Status:** desk research; not legal advice, supplier verification or a manufacturing audit.

## Executive finding

There is evidence for two distinct China connections:

1. **Patent protection:** Chinese invention patent [CN115003190B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN115003190B/en), application CN202180011338.5, was published as CN115003190A on 2 September 2022 and granted/published as CN115003190B on 9 April 2024. Its priority date is 31 January 2020.
2. **Tooling and initial production:** a 15 October 2025 profile in *Le Journal de Mayotte* reports that Youmna Mouhamad travelled to China in 2024 to initiate manufacture of an industrial mould and begin initial production. The article reports a mould cost estimate of €150,000–€200,000 and a €50,000 grant from the Agence Nationale pour la Recherche et le Développement.

These are not the same kind of evidence. The patent is a published patent record. The China manufacturing account is a secondary-media report and has not yet been independently corroborated with a factory identity, contract, invoice, shipment record, tooling photograph or company statement naming the supplier.

**Do not state that a particular Chinese factory, city, province or supplier has been identified. None has been established in this research.**

### Technical subject matter

The patent describes a combined hair-combing and product-dispensing device. Its disclosed architecture includes:

- a body with a reservoir for hair-treatment substance;
- comb teeth arranged in a row;
- a rotatable roller that transfers substance from the reservoir to hair;
- a wall with a concave inner surface supporting the roller and a convex outer surface carrying the teeth; and
- arcuate through-slots between teeth through which the roller can contact hair and dispense product.

The description discusses thicker creams and pastes, roller texture and circumferential bands, smooth sealing regions, removable/cleanable components and housing arrangements. These disclosures are useful for engineering interpretation. They do not establish that every described embodiment appears in the retail product.

### Example dimensions disclosed in the description

| Feature | Disclosed example | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Tooth spacing | at least 5 mm; example around 10 mm | Patent embodiment guidance, not a verified retail measurement |
| Roller radius | approximately 10–30 mm | Disclosed range, not a measured production specification |
| Roller projections | approximately 1–3 mm high | Disclosed example, not verified against a physical sample |

**Claim-scope caution:** the description contains many optional embodiments. Legal protection is determined by the granted claims interpreted under the relevant law, not by treating every paragraph or drawing as independently protected. A claim chart should be prepared from the official granted Chinese claims before making infringement or freedom-to-operate statements.

### Legal status caution

Google Patents displays an automated status label. It is a useful discovery interface, not an official legal-status certificate. Check the CNIPA patent register for annual-fee payment, lapse, restoration, assignment, invalidation or other legal events before relying on the patent as currently enforceable.

Official verification route: [CNIPA patent search/service portal](https://cponline.cnipa.gov.cn/).

## 7. CAD and product-engineering implications

The Chinese patent provides a basis for an **illustrative, evidence-linked reconstruction**, not a manufacturing-ready model.

Recommended component decomposition:

1. Reservoir / main housing.
2. Dispensing wall with concave roller seat.
3. Roller body and end spindles.
4. Circumferential texture bands and smooth sealing regions.
5. Comb teeth.
6. Arcuate dispensing slots.
7. Refill / access cover and retention features.

### Parameter provenance

Every CAD parameter should carry one of these labels:

- **PATENT-DISCLOSED:** a value explicitly stated in the patent.
- **MEASURED:** measured from an authorised physical sample with instrument, method and uncertainty recorded.
- **IMAGE-ESTIMATED:** estimated from a published image; low confidence unless calibrated.
- **ENGINEERING-ASSUMPTION:** chosen to make a parametric model solvable; not claimed as product truth.
- **SUPPLIER-CONFIRMED:** supported by authorised tooling or manufacturing documentation.

Do not use patent example dimensions as though they were verified production dimensions. Do not infer wall thickness, draft angle, shrinkage allowance, resin grade, gate location, ejector layout, tolerances or cavity count without evidence.

### Validation sequence

1. Validate source and parameter provenance.
2. Check component interfaces and assembly clearances.
3. Check roller rotation and contact with the dispensing slots.
4. Model leakage/sealing hypotheses separately from proven performance.
5. Test dispensing with representative fluid viscosities only under a defined, safe test protocol.
6. Compare reconstructed geometry with an authorised physical sample if one becomes available.
7. Keep visual similarity, functional performance and manufacturing readiness as separate validation gates.

The existing Sand3D experiment should continue to treat Sand3D as an independent third-party project. Use documented adapters, pin the tested version, preserve notices and record API/runtime compatibility. A generated mesh is not validated engineering CAD.

## 8. Commercial and strategic observations

The reported China tooling step is strategically significant because it suggests the venture moved beyond prototype design into capital-intensive industrialisation. The mould is a potentially critical asset: ownership, access, maintenance, portability and supplier exclusivity could materially affect manufacturing resilience and bargaining power.

The Mayotte article also reports an ambition to develop haircare products using local plants. Treat this as a reported product-development direction, not evidence that a formulation range has launched or that ingredient sourcing, safety assessment or claims substantiation are complete.

Potential diligence themes:

- hardware-plus-formulation product ecosystem;
- cultural provenance and ingredient traceability;
- tooling ownership and supplier concentration;
- IP enforcement and territorial coverage;
- quality assurance across contract manufacturing;
- packaging, refillability, cleaning and product lifecycle;
- distribution evidence versus stated target markets.
